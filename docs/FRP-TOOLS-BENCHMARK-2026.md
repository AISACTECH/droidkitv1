## 0 · Method law (read before the numbers)

1. **This repo's tool is scored by executing its real engine** — `computeBand`,
   `buildAdaptivePlan`, `evaluateParallelLanes`, `LAB_LEDGER` — against the corpus below.
2. **Competitors are scored by a documented-capability model**: deterministic tables distilled
   from public evidence (FRP-ALGORITHM-ANALYSIS.md, RESEARCH-2026-FRP.md, COMPARISON-2026-FINAL.md,
   the A15/16 patch dossier). Their binaries are **not executed here**; model gates
   (brand support, model lists, server-route availability) are applied explicitly.
3. **No randomness.** Same inputs → byte-identical output; the input hash above pins the inputs.
4. **Bands, not promises.** Every success figure is an evidence band; ours never exceeds 97.
   The generator self-audits the document for banned marketing phrases before writing it.
5. **The honest limit:** true head-to-head telemetry requires a bench with real devices and
   licensed copies of commercial tools — declared as the next gate, not hidden.

## 9 · The verdict — who outperforms whom, honestly

**Overall composite winner: DroidKit v1 (AISACTECH — this repo)** at 87.6/100 — driven by the axes commercial tools don't compete on (decision coverage, safety, honesty, openness), not by pretending to win the raw-success race.
**Raw success winner: DroidKit v1 (AISACTECH — this repo)** at 69/97 — scored by RUNNING the real engine: below-OS chipset lanes (Brom/SPD/EDL/Download-Mode) dominate this Kenya-market-heavy corpus, each rate sourced from the lab ledger.
**Android 15/16 raw leader: this repo** at 64.3/97. Below-OS lanes (Brom/SPD) are the honest differentiator — they sit below the patch wall.

**Where this repo wins outright:** decision coverage (100%, tested — every fingerprint gets a measured band, ranked chain, verification, rollback and journal), safety (refusal gates, dump-first law, recovery scripts), honesty (bands capped at 97, visible refusals, banned-claim self-audit), openness (user-runnable test suites), and price ($0, MIT).
**Where commercial/bench tools still lead (declared, not hidden):** paid server/IMEI routes on supported Samsung A15/16, signed firehose loaders and EDL cables on the bench, and beginner polish/call-centre support. Native hardware execution for this repo is roadmapped (RFCs), bench-gated, never shipped unverified.
**The physics floor:** on a fully-patched A15/16 device with an unknown chipset and no pre-authorized ADB (e.g. the Pixel 9 corpus row), every tool scores —/0 — the lock is server-side. The difference is that this repo is the one that prints the refusal.
